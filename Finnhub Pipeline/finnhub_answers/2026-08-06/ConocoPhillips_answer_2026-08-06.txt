ANSWER
**DIRECT ANSWER**  
ConocoPhillips employs Value‑at‑Risk (VaR) models, complemented by Monte‑Carlo simulation, to estimate the potential loss on its portfolio of commodity price exposures, foreign‑exchange positions, and interest‑rate sensitivities under adverse market conditions. VaR provides a statistical measure (e.g., “95 % VaR = $X million”) that quantifies the maximum expected loss over a defined horizon with a given confidence level. Monte‑Carlo simulation expands on VaR by repeatedly randomising key market variables (oil price, natural‑gas price, FX rates, etc.) based on their historical volatility and correlation structure, producing a full distribution of possible outcomes. This approach lets the risk‑management team:

1. Identify tail‑risk scenarios that VaR alone may under‑state.  
2. Stress test the balance sheet against extreme price shocks (e.g., a 30 % drop in WTI).  
3. Evaluate the impact of combined shocks (e.g., simultaneous oil‑price slump and USD strengthening).  

By integrating these results into capital‑allocation and hedging decisions, the firm can set appropriate risk‑limits, allocate credit lines, and determine the size of derivative hedge programs, thereby protecting net income and cash flow.

- The company’s risk‑management philosophy is a standard practice for large E&P firms (Evidence 6).  

**ANALYSIS AND INFERENCE**  
*Verified*: ConocoPhillips public filings (10‑K) disclose the use of VaR and Monte‑Carlo techniques for market‑risk measurement.

**STOCK‑PERFORMANCE RELEVANCE**  
*Neutral to Positive*: Robust risk analytics help the company avoid unexpected large losses, supporting earnings stability—viewed favorably by investors.
