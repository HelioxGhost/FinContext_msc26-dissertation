ANSWER
**DIRECT ANSWER**  
ConocoPhillips uses a Value‑at‑Risk (VaR) framework, supplemented by Monte Carlo simulation, to quantify its exposure to adverse market movements such as swings in crude‑oil, natural‑gas, foreign‑exchange, and interest‑rate markets. VaR provides a statistical estimate (e.g., at the 95 % confidence level) of the maximum expected loss over a set horizon (typically one day or one month). Monte Carlo simulation expands on VaR by generating thousands of random price‑path scenarios for the underlying commodities and currencies, allowing the company to:

* Capture non‑linear relationships and tail‑risk events that a simple historical VaR may miss.  
* Stress‑test the balance sheet under extreme price shocks (e.g., a 30 % drop in oil price).  
* Assess the effectiveness of hedging programs by comparing simulated unhedged vs. hedged loss distributions.  

The output informs capital‑allocation decisions, hedge sizing, and disclosures in the annual “risk management” section of the Form 10‑K.

**ANALYSIS AND INFERENCE**  
*Verified:* Use of VaR and Monte Carlo is a common risk‑management approach for integrated oil companies and is referenced in ConocoPhillips’ public disclosures.  
*Inference:* The models give the firm a quantitative view of potential losses, guiding hedging and capital‑reserve policies, which in turn impacts earnings volatility and investor confidence.

**STOCK‑PERFORMANCE RELEVANCE**
