VaR provides a statistical estimate (e.g., a 95 % confidence level) of the maximum expected loss over a defined time horizon (typically one day or 10 days) under normal market conditions.  Monte Carlo simulation expands this by repeatedly modeling thousands of random price paths for key variables—crude oil, natural‑gas, foreign‑exchange rates, and interest rates—to capture tail‑risk events and non‑linear relationships (e.g., option‑like exposure in hedges).  Together, they allow the firm to:

* Identify the size of potential downside under stressed price scenarios.  
* Stress‑test the adequacy of capital reserves and hedging programs.  
* Inform treasury decisions on hedge ratios and credit‑line sizing.

**ANALYSIS AND INFERENCE**  
*Verified evidence*: Absence of disclosed VaR numbers; presence of beta metric.  
*Inference*: Large integrated oil producers routinely report VaR and Monte Carlo results in their 10‑K/10‑Q filings.  ConocoPhillips’s low beta indicates modest equity‑price volatility, which, when fed into VaR calculations, likely yields a relatively modest dollar VaR figure (e.g., low‑single‑digit millions).
