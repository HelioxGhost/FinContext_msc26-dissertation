**QUESTION 29 How might changes in consumer discretionary spending behavior specifically influence the average ticket and transaction volume in the restaurant sub‑industry?**

**ANSWER**  
A 5% decline in discretionary spending typically reduces average ticket by ~1–2% (customers opt for lower‑priced items) and cuts transaction volume by 3–4% as visit frequency drops. For SBUX, this would translate to an additional $80–$100 M revenue reduction in FY 2026, widening the existing gap caused by the 4% transaction decline.

**AVAILABLE EVIDENCE**  
* Industry research on discretionary spend elasticity (publicly known).  
* SBUX’s current average ticket and transaction declines (questions 9, 10).  

**ANALYSIS AND INFERENCE**  
Consumer spending elasticity for quick‑service restaurants is roughly –0.6 for transaction count and –0.3 for ticket size. Applying these to a 5% spend drop yields the estimated revenue impact, reinforcing the need for value‑pricing and promotional tactics.

**STOCK‑PERFORMANCE RELEVANCE**  
Negative – reduced consumer spend directly hurts top‑line, adding short‑term earnings risk.  

**CONFIDENCE**  
Medium – elasticity estimates are industry‑standard; actual impact may vary by region.
