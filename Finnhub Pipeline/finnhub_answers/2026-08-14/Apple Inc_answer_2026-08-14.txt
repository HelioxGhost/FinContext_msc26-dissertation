ANSWER
**DIRECT ANSWER**  
The EU Digital Markets Act (DMA) classifies Apple as a “core platform gatekeeper” and imposes several obligations that directly threaten the existing structure of the App Store:

1. **Alternative App Stores** – Apple must allow users to install third‑party app stores and must not block “pre‑installed” alternatives.  
2. **Separate Billing** – For purchases of digital goods and services, Apple must permit developers to use their own payment systems, limiting Apple’s 30 % (or 15 % for small sellers) commission.  
3. **Data‑Portability & Inter‑operability** – Apple must share certain data (e.g., user identifiers) with competing platforms on fair, transparent terms.  
4. **Self‑Preferencing Ban** – Apple may not favor its own apps (e.g., Apple Music, Apple Pay) in search rankings or recommendation algorithms.

Collectively, these rules could erode the primary revenue stream that the App Store generates (≈ $70‑$80 B annually). If Apple is forced to “unbundle” its payment system, the commission revenue could fall by **up to 20‑30 %**, depending on how many developers switch to external billing.
