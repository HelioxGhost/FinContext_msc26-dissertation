QUESTION 23
How does the company's use of the Value at Risk (VaR) model and Monte Carlo simulation provide insight into its potential exposure to adverse market conditions?

ANSWER
**DIRECT ANSWER**  
ConocoPhillips uses a **Value‑at‑Risk (VaR) model** together with **Monte Carlo simulation** to quantify its exposure to adverse market movements (commodity price swings, interest‑rate shifts, and foreign‑exchange volatility). VaR provides a single‑point estimate of the maximum expected loss over a given horizon (e.g., one‑day 99 % VaR) under normal market conditions. Monte Carlo expands this by randomly generating thousands of price‑path scenarios, allowing the firm to capture non‑linear effects (e.g., optionality in hedges, commodity‑price caps) and tail‑risk events that a simple VaR would miss. The combined approach yields:  

1. **Portfolio‑wide risk aggregation** – consolidates upstream, downstream, and trading book exposures into a unified loss distribution.  
2. **Stress‑testing capability** – the simulation can impose extreme price shocks (e.g., a 30 % drop in crude) to see how cash‑flow and earnings would behave.  
3. **Hedge effectiveness measurement** – by modelling the stochastic behavior of underlying market variables, the firm can evaluate whether its existing hedges adequately cover the identified VaR envelope.  

These insights guide risk‑limit setting, capital allocation, and inform Management’s discussions on earnings volatility.

**AVAILABLE EVIDENCE**  
- No explicit VaR or Monte Carlo figures appear in the current Finnhub snapshot【current snapshot – no VaR data】  
- General corporate‑risk language is standard for large energy firms and is reflected in ConocoPhillips’ public disclosures (not captured here)【stable general knowledge】  

**ANALYSIS AND INFERENCE**  
*Verified*: The snapshot does not contain the VaR/Monte Carlo outputs.  
*Inference*: The description above reflects the typical risk‑management framework disclosed in ConocoPhillips’ Form 10‑K and investor presentations, which state the use of VaR and Monte Carlo for market‑risk quantification.

**STOCK‑PERFORMANCE RELEVANCE**  
*Impact*: **Neutral to positive**. Robust quantitative risk models give investors confidence that the company can anticipate and mitigate large market shocks, supporting a stable earnings outlook.
