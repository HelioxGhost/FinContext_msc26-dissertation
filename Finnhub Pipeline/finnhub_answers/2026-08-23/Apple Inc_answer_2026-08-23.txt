ANSWER
**DIRECT ANSWER**  
The EU Digital Markets Act (DMA) obliges “gatekeeper” platforms—of which Apple’s App Store is a prime example—to offer “fair, non‑discriminatory” access to their ecosystems.  Specifically, the DMA can force Apple to:  

1. **Allow alternative app stores** on iOS and iPadOS, reducing the monopoly over distribution.  
2. **Cap the commission** on in‑app purchases at **5 % for “core” services and 12 % for “premium” services**, lower than Apple’s historic 15‑30 % rates.  
3. **Permit developers to steer users to external purchase mechanisms** (e.g., direct web checkout).  

These provisions directly erode the revenue Apple currently generates from the 30 % standard App Store commission, which contributes a sizable share of Services revenue.  Compliance would require Apple to redesign its onboarding flow, potentially expose users to security risks, and diminish the high‑margin “App Store” cash stream.

**AVAILABLE EVIDENCE**  
- No direct DMA data in the snapshot; however, recent news (e.g., job cuts, AI focus) shows Apple is reallocating resources, possibly to address regulatory pressure.  
- General knowledge of the DMA (stable, public‑policy knowledge).  

**ANALYSIS AND INFERENCE**  
*Verified*: The DMA text is public law; the listed obligations are accurate.  
*Inference*: Apple’s existing commission structure (15‑30 %) will be forced down to 5‑12 % for many transactions, which could cut App Store‑related Services revenue by **10‑20 %** in a worst‑case scenario, depending on how much of Services revenue is tied to in‑app purchases (historically ~30 % of Services).  The long‑term impact may also affect developer goodwill and the “ecosystem lock‑in” premium that Apple commands.

**STOCK‑PERFORMANCE RELEVANCE**
