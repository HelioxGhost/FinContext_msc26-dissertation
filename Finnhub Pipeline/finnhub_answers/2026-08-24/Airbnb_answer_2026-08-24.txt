1. **Index Inclusion** – Most major indices (e.g., S&P 500, MSCI World) require that the share class used for weighting be the **primary voting‑right class**. If the float‑adjusted market cap of the voting class is lower than the total market cap, an index provider may apply an “adjusted‑float” methodology, which can slightly reduce Airbnb’s weighting relative to a single‑class peer.

2. **Passive Flows** – ETFs and index funds typically hold the **most liquid, voting‑right series**. Because Airbnb’s floating share count (413.17 M) already reflects the tradable portion of its Class A shares, the existence of a non‑voting class does not materially impede passive fund ownership, but it can create **minor tracking‑error differences** for funds that must exclude the non‑voting series.

Overall, the multi‑series structure is **unlikely to be a barrier** to inclusion in large U.S. indices, but it may lead to a modestly lower index weight and could cause some passive managers to adjust holdings to match the voting‑right float.
