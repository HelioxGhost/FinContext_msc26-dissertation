The DMA obliges Apple to (i) allow alternative app stores on iOS, (ii) offer developers the ability to direct users to external payment systems, and (iii) share certain non‑public data with competitors. These requirements directly threaten Apple’s current App Store model, which charges a **30 % commission** on most digital goods and services and controls the sole distribution channel for iOS apps.

**AVAILABLE EVIDENCE**  
- No specific DMA‑related news in the current Finnhub feed, but the regulatory framework is well‑documented in public EU policy.  
- General company news includes litigation activity (AI trade‑secret suit) indicating a heightened legal focus, suggesting Apple is already managing multiple regulatory exposures.  

**ANALYSIS AND INFERENCE**  
1. **Commission Pressure:** DMA could force Apple to lower the standard 30 % fee to a level comparable with competitors (e.g., 15 %‑20 %).  
2. **Alternative Stores:** Allowing third‑party app stores would erode Apple’s exclusive control, potentially reducing overall app‑store transaction volume and the associated service‑revenue share.  
3. **Data Sharing:** Mandatory data access for rivals could diminish Apple’s competitive advantage in user‑behavior analytics, affecting the personalization that drives higher‑margin services.  
4. **Compliance Costs:** Implementing technical and legal changes to satisfy DMA could increase operating expenses in the short term.  

**STOCK‑PERFORMANCE RELEVANCE**  
*Negative to mixed, medium‑term.* A reduction in App Store revenue (which contributed **≈ 20 %** of total net sales in recent years) would weigh on earnings growth.
