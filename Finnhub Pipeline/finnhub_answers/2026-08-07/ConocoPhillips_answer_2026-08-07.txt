QUESTION 16
How do the company's commodity price risk management strategies, specifically the use of swap contracts, affect its exposure to fixed-price versus floating-price sales?

ANSWER
**DIRECT ANSWER**  
ConocoPhillips uses interest‑rate‑ and commodity‑price swap contracts to hedge the bulk of its upstream production, converting a large portion of its floating‑price oil and gas sales into fixed‑price cash flows. The swaps lock in a predetermined price (or price range) for a set volume of commodity, which dampens the effect of spot‑price swings on earnings. Consequently, the company’s exposure to market price volatility is shifted from the “downside” of floating sales to the contractual obligations of the swaps; when spot prices fall below the swap strike, the firm receives the higher fixed price, while when spot prices rise above the strike the gains are limited to the swap‑defined upside. The net effect is a smoother earnings profile and a more predictable cash‑flow stream, albeit at the cost of forgoing some upside in strong price environments.
