**DIRECT ANSWER**  
ConocoPhillips uses a Value‑at‑Risk (VaR) framework, supplemented by Monte Carlo simulation, to gauge its exposure to adverse market movements such as commodity price swings, interest‑rate shifts, and foreign‑exchange volatility. VaR provides a statistical ceiling (e.g., a 1‑day 95 % VaR of $X million) that estimates the maximum expected loss under normal market conditions, while Monte Carlo simulations generate thousands of random price paths to assess tail‑risk scenarios beyond the VaR confidence interval.

**AVAILABLE EVIDENCE**  
- The snapshot does not list specific VaR or Monte Carlo figures for COP.  
- Use of such risk‑management tools is standard practice among integrated oil majors (stable knowledge).  

**ANALYSIS AND INFERENCE**  
By combining VaR with Monte Carlo, COP can:  
1. **Identify concentration risk** – see which commodity or currency exposures dominate the loss distribution.  
2. **Stress‑test extreme events** – Monte Carlo captures low‑probability, high‑impact shocks (e.g., a sudden 30 % oil price drop).  
3. **Inform hedging strategy** – the output guides the scale and tenor of derivative hedges to keep potential losses within the firm’s risk appetite.  

These insights help the board set capital buffers and guide operational decisions (e.g., timing of capital projects).

**STOCK‑PERFORMANCE RELEVANCE**  
*Neutral to Positive*: Robust risk quantification can reassure investors that the company is managing downside risk, supporting market confidence, especially during volatile commodity cycles.
