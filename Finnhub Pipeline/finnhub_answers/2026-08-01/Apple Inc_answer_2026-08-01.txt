1. **Reduced commission rates** – the DMA caps “core platform services” fees at 5 % (or 12 % for “strategic” services) versus Apple’s standard 30 % take.  
2. **Interoperability** – Apple must allow “sharing” of app‑store data with competing “alternative” stores, effectively opening the iOS ecosystem to third‑party payment and distribution channels.  
3. **Non‑discriminatory access** – Apple cannot favor its own apps (e.g., Apple TV+, Apple Music) over rivals in search ranking or visibility.  

If enforced, these provisions would erode the high‑margin “30 %” revenue stream that contributes roughly 20‑25 % of App Store gross fees, compressing operating margins on services and potentially increasing compliance costs (e.g., system redesign, legal defenses).

**ANALYSIS AND INFERENCE**

*Inference*: Assuming a 20 % reduction in App Store fee revenue (from 30 % to ~24 % average after DMA caps), Apple’s annual services revenue (~$78 bn in FY 2025) could lose roughly $1.5‑$2 bn in gross fees, translating to a 0.4‑0.5 % hit to overall operating margin.
